The Vikings Never Vanished — You Inherited Them

13 August 2026 · 12 min watch · hand-drawn animated history

The short answer

Everyone says the Vikings vanished in one bloody week in 1066. They didn't — they won, wearing a new name. This is the real story of the Viking Age (793–1066): the raids, the longships, the voyage to America 471 years before Columbus, and the twist that the men who "ended" the Vikings were Vikings themselves.

The story

The Vikings, explained. Who the Vikings really were, why the Viking Age began with the raid on Lindisfarne in 793, how the Great Heathen Army nearly swallowed England, and why it "ended" at Stamford Bridge and Hastings in 1066. We cover Harald Hardrada, Alfred the Great and the Danelaw, Rollo and Normandy, Erik the Red and Leif Erikson reaching Vinland (North America) in 1021, Cnut the Great's North Sea empire, and Harald Bluetooth — plus how the Normans, and every English monarch since, carry Viking blood. And the myths: no, they never wore horned helmets, and yes, you still speak dozens of their words and name four days of your week after their gods.
