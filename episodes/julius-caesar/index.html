The short answer

Julius Caesar was never emperor of Rome, yet more than sixty senators plotted to kill him and stabbed him twenty-three times. The sourced history of the man who built your calendar: the pirates, the debts, Gaul and Alesia, the Rubicon, the coin with his living face, the crown he refused, the Ides of March, and what the daggers actually saved.

Julius Caesar explained: the ancient history and military history of the late Roman Republic, from Caesar's kidnapping by pirates and the debts Crassus guaranteed to the First Triumvirate with Pompey, the conquest of Gaul, the Rhine bridge, the siege of Alesia and Vercingetorix, crossing the Rubicon in 49 BC, the Battle of Pharsalus, Pompey's death in Egypt, Cleopatra and Caesarion, the Julian calendar reform of 46 BC, the CAESAR DICT PERPETVO denarius, the Lupercalia diadem, the assassination of Julius Caesar on the Ides of March 44 BC by Brutus and Cassius, the will that made Octavian his heir, the fates of the assassins, and how killing Caesar ended the Roman Republic and created the first Roman emperor, Augustus.
