Greek Myths That Were Real, and the "Facts" We Invented

19 November 2026 · 60 min watch · hand-drawn animated history

▶

The short answer

Which Greek myths were real, and which famous "facts" about Ancient Greece were invented? A one-hour dig through sixteen layers of time, from the Olympic torch relay (invented in 1936) down to the Bronze Age town buried by the eruption of Thera, with a verdict on every story: real, half-real, invented or still disputed.

The story

Greek mythology and ancient history, myth versus archaeology: the Olympic torch relay and the 1936 Berlin Olympics, why Greek statues look white (painted statues and polychromy), Pandora's box versus Pandora's jar (Hesiod and Erasmus), the Colossus of Rhodes, Archimedes' death ray and the siege of Syracuse, the legend of the marathon runner Pheidippides, Achilles' heel, Pluto's Gate (the Ploutonion of Hierapolis), Diogenes and the barrel, who could vote in Athenian democracy, the bones of Orestes and ancient fossil hunting, the Oracle of the Dead, King Midas' tomb at Gordion, the Chimera's eternal flames at Yanartas, the Lefkandi centaur, and the Minoan eruption of Thera and the archaeology of Akrotiri on Santorini: ancient Greek civilization, one verdict at a time. Interest in Greek myth renewed by Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey (2026) and Percy Jackson and the Olympians.
