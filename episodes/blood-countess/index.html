Did Elizabeth Bathory Really Bathe in Blood?

The short answer

You can watch Elizabeth Báthory, the Blood Countess, bathe in blood on Netflix. But while she lived, more than 300 witnesses were questioned about her, and no one mentioned a bath: it first appears in print in 1729, 115 years after she died. This is the trial she never got, four centuries late, with the oldest records answering the legend's charges one at a time.

The story

Elizabeth Báthory (Báthory Erzsébet), the Hungarian countess called the Blood Countess or 'Countess Dracula', is often named the most prolific female serial killer in history. Did she really bathe in blood? Was she walled up alive at Csejte (Čachtice) Castle? Where does the famous number of 650 victims come from, and was she framed by greedy nobles? With the Hungarian National Archives and the historians who reread the 1610–1611 testimony, we test each charge: the investigation ordered by the palatine György Thurzó, the Christmas raid, the four servants tortured and three of them executed in 1611, the castle arrest, the witnesses, and the Jesuit history of 1729 where the blood bath first appears. Early modern European history and Hungarian history, told as a trial. Interest renewed by Netflix's Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story (2026).
