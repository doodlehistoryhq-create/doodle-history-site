Why Did Pompeii's Victims Die in One Second? (Vesuvius 79 AD)

30 August 2026 · 11 min watch · hand-drawn animated history

▶

The short answer

In 79 AD, Mount Vesuvius buried the Roman city of Pompeii in a single day. But almost everything we were taught about how its people died is wrong. This is the true story of Pompeii and the eruption of Vesuvius: an ordinary Roman town, the warnings it ignored, and the forensic science of what really happened in its final hours.

The story

A full history of Pompeii and the 79 AD eruption of Mount Vesuvius, explained. How did the people of Pompeii really die? Why are the famous plaster casts not actually bodies? How did most of the city escape, and who was left behind? We follow the only eyewitness account, written by Pliny the Younger, the death of his uncle Pliny the Elder, the buried town of Herculaneum, the pyroclastic surges, and why the eruption date, traditionally the 24th of August, may really have been in October. Plus why three million people still live in the shadow of Vesuvius today. This is ancient history and archaeology at their most human: a Roman city, a volcanic eruption, and the modern excavation science of plaster casts, scans and digs that finally explained what happened.
