The Byzantine Empire Never Existed (Rome Didn't Fall in 476)

1 October 2026 · 12 min watch · hand-drawn animated history

▶

The short answer

The Byzantine Empire is the greatest plot twist in history: the Roman Empire did NOT fall in 476 AD — it survived for roughly another thousand years, and its citizens called themselves Romans the entire time.

The story

This is the Byzantine Empire explained: why the Eastern Roman Empire survived when the West collapsed, Justinian and Theodora and the Nika riots, the building of the Hagia Sophia, Greek fire and the Theodosian Walls, the Fourth Crusade's sack of Constantinople in 1204, and the fall of Constantinople in 1453 — Mehmed the Conqueror's giant cannon, ships hauled overland, and Constantine XI, the last Roman emperor, dying in the breach. Plus the answer to how did the Byzantine Empire fall, and why the name "Byzantine" was only invented in 1557. A thousand years of Roman military history: the sieges of Constantinople, Greek fire at sea, the Theodosian Walls, and the cannon that ended the medieval world in 1453.
