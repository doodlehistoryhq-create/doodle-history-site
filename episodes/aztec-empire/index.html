What Really Destroyed the Aztec Empire? (Not Spain)

30 July 2026 · 13 min watch · hand-drawn animated history

▶

The short answer

The Aztec Empire built the biggest city in the Americas and ruled several million people, then fell in just two years. But the story that a few hundred Spaniards toppled it is a myth. This is the real history of the rise and fall of the Aztec Empire, and what really destroyed it.

The story

How did the Aztec Empire fall? The Aztecs, or Mexica, founded Tenochtitlan on a lake around 1325, forged the Triple Alliance in 1428 under Itzcoatl and Tlacaelel, and built a vast tribute empire crowned by the Templo Mayor. This is the Aztec Empire explained: the truth about human sacrifice, Moctezuma the Second, Hernan Cortes and Malintzin, the Tlaxcalan alliance, the smallpox epidemic, and the 93-day siege that ended with the fall of Tenochtitlan on 13 August 1521 — a conquest won by the Aztecs' own indigenous enemies, disease, and Spanish steel, not by a handful of conquistadors. A history of Mesoamerican civilization and conquest: Tenochtitlan's rise from a swamp, the war with Cortés, and the archaeology of the skull tower that revised the story.
