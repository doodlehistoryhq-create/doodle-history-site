Ancient Egypt: 3,000 Years Nothing Could Conquer

The short answer

For five hundred years, Ancient Egypt wasn't a land of quiet tombs — it was the most powerful war machine on Earth, an empire whose chariots ran from deep in Sudan to the Euphrates. This is the complete rise and fall of Ancient Egypt: how the longest-lived empire in history conquered the ancient world, survived the catastrophe that ended the Bronze Age, and was finally beaten not by a stronger army — but by time itself.

The story

Ancient Egypt explained — from the pyramids and the pharaohs to Hatshepsut, the battle of Megiddo, and Cleopatra, this is the story of the longest-lived civilization in human history: three thousand years of empire on the Nile, and how it finally fell to Rome. A military and social history of ancient Egyptian civilization: the pharaohs, the invasions it outlasted for three thousand years, and the archaeology that rebuilt its story.
