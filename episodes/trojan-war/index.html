The Trojan War: How Much of It Was Actually Real?

The short answer

The Trojan War is the most famous story in history that nobody could prove. So we went through it claim by claim and ruled on each one: the wooden horse, the thousand ships, the ten-year siege, Priam's gold, and the city itself. Some of it collapses the moment you touch it. One piece survives in a place nobody was even looking.

The story

Did the Trojan War really happen? This is the Trojan War explained as a verdict rather than a retelling: how much of Homer's Iliad is history, how much is myth, and what archaeology at the mound of Hisarlik in north-west Turkey can and cannot prove. We cover why the Trojan horse is not in the Iliad at all, why the face that launched a thousand ships is Christopher Marlowe and not Homer, why Frank Calvert and not Heinrich Schliemann worked out where Troy was, why Priam's Treasure came from the wrong city by roughly a thousand years, why Bronze Age Troy was Anatolian rather than Greek, what the burn layer of Troy VIIa actually shows, and what the Hittite clay tablets from Hattusa say about a city called Wilusa, a king called Alaksandu and a people called the Ahhiyawa. Troy myth or reality, the real history behind the Trojan War, and the honest scholarly verdict on whether the Trojan War was real. With Christopher Nolan's film The Odyssey (2026) renewing worldwide interest in Homer's epics, this is the ancient history and archaeology behind the legend: Bronze Age warfare, the excavations at Troy from Schliemann onward, and what the Hittite records actually say.
