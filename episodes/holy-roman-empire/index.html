The Holy Roman Empire: The 'Joke' That Lasted 1,000 Years

26 November 2026 · 11 min watch · hand-drawn animated history

▶

The short answer

The Holy Roman Empire was neither holy, nor Roman, nor an empire. That is Voltaire's famous joke, and the empire it mocked lasted about a thousand years. We put the joke on trial, one word at a time: a Frankish king crowned in Rome on Christmas Day 800, a king barefoot at the gate of Canossa in midwinter, an imperial election two-thirds paid for by one banker, and an emperor who signed the whole thing away himself in Vienna in 1806.

The story

The Holy Roman Empire explained: why it was called holy, Roman and an empire, and how it really worked. Charlemagne's coronation in 800 and Otto I's in 962, Empress Irene and the 'vacant' throne in Constantinople, Henry IV at Canossa in 1077 and the investiture controversy, Frederick II excommunicated again and again, the Golden Bull of 1356 and the seven electors, the 1519 election that Jakob Fugger's money won for Charles V, the Thirty Years' War and the Peace of Westphalia, Austerlitz, the Confederation of the Rhine and Francis II's abdication in 1806. Then the reassessment: the Imperial Chamber Court, the Perpetual Diet at Regensburg, the Imperial Crown in Vienna, and why historians such as Peter H. Wilson read it on its own terms. Medieval history, early modern history and European history, from the sources below.
