The Inca Empire Wasn't Conquered — It Destroyed Itself

6 August 2026 · 11 min watch · hand-drawn animated history

▶

The short answer

The Inca Empire was the largest state the Americas ever built — around ten million people, forty thousand kilometres of mountain road, no wheel and no writing — yet it fell to fewer than two hundred Spaniards in barely a year. This is the real history of how the Inca Empire didn't so much get conquered as collapse from the inside.

The story

How did the Inca Empire fall so fast? Here is the Inca Empire explained end to end: the rise of Tawantinsuyu under Pachacuti, the god-emperor system of the Sapa Inca, the smallpox epidemic that killed Huayna Capac and triggered the civil war between Atahualpa and Huascar, and the Battle of Cajamarca in 1532, where Francisco Pizarro captured the emperor Atahualpa. We cover the Atahualpa ransom and the room of gold, his execution in 1533, the Spanish conquest of Peru, and the last Inca holdout at Vilcabamba until 1572.
