Which Greek Myths Actually Happened?

27 September 2026 · 15 min watch · hand-drawn animated history

▶

The short answer

For roughly four hundred years nobody in Greece could write. Everything the Greeks knew about their own Bronze Age had to cross that gap inside songs — and a few things made it. This is Greek mythology tested against the archaeological record: what the evidence actually confirms, and the exact point where each story stops being true.

The story

Greek mythology explained, and how much of it was really real. This episode of ancient history walks the hard evidence myth by myth: the boar's-tusk helmet Homer describes in the Iliad and archaeologists dug out of Mycenaean graves; "Mycenae rich in gold" and Heinrich Schliemann's 1876 shaft graves; the name Achilles on a Linear B clay tablet after Michael Ventris cracked the script; the "Mistress of the Labyrinth" tablet from Knossos and what it does not prove about the Minotaur; the Amazons and the Scythian warrior women found buried with weapons and combat injuries on the steppe; the Oracle of Delphi and the fault lines under the temple of Apollo; and the Golden Fleece explained by Strabo as sheepskins catching river gold in Colchis. It closes on two famous archaeology stories that do not hold up — the griffin and the Cyclops. Covering Bronze Age civilization, military history and the archaeology of the Trojan War era, with sources for every claim. Interest in Greek myth has been renewed by Christopher Nolan's film The Odyssey (2026).
